ROOMFORGE FIELD GUIDE / 01

Flooring math,
made beautifully simple.

Planning a new floor? Before buying a single box, here’s how to turn accurate measurements into a realistic materials list.

01. Calculate your floor area

For a rectangular room, multiply the interior width by the interior length. Measure in feet for square feet, or meters for square meters. Always measure the finished floor space rather than estimating from furniture placement.

Floor area = width × length

For a room that is 12 ft wide and 18 ft long, the floor area is 216 ft².

02. Include a waste allowance

Some material is lost to cutting, offcuts and unexpected damage. A planning allowance of 5–15% may be useful for straightforward jobs, but the right percentage depends on room shape, installation pattern, material and installer guidance. This calculator allows you to choose the allowance.

Required coverage = floor area × (1 + waste % ÷ 100)

At 10% allowance: 216 × 1.10 = 237.6 ft².

03. Convert area into whole boxes

Flooring is typically sold in boxes with a coverage specification. Divide the required coverage by the coverage printed on the box, then always round up to the next whole box.

Boxes = round up (required coverage ÷ coverage per box)

With 24 ft² per box: 237.6 ÷ 24 = 9.9, so you need 10 boxes.

04. Estimate your materials budget

Multiply whole boxes by the price of each box. Apply sales tax separately if needed. Prices can vary by store, grade, location and promotional availability. Installation and other supplies must be budgeted separately.

Material cost = boxes × price per box

Ten boxes at $45 each means $450 before tax and additional materials.

What the number doesn’t include

A flooring estimate is not a substitute for a site visit or installation plan. This first version assumes a rectangular room and uses a percentage-based waste allowance. It does not optimize individual cuts, model irregular spaces, or include trims, transitions, underlayment, adhesive, prep, delivery, labor or tool rental.

For stairs, alcoves, angled walls or complicated tile designs, verify quantities with your supplier or installer.
